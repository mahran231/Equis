# EQUIS NUTRITION — Plan Design DNA
Version 1.0 · 24 September 2026

## Authority and scope

Use the final revised **EQUIS_Rahma_Plan_v3_Approved.pdf** as the visual master: green cover profile card; green quotation card; revised nutrition-target page; portion-measurement box removed from the weekly overview. Earlier PDFs and unedited writing-block drafts do not override this final edition.

This specification applies to future EQUIS client nutrition, training and progress documents. Preserve the visual system while calculating and writing each client's content individually. New client plans retain the mandatory 20-question intake. Do not copy Rahma's personal details, measurements, calorie targets or meal quantities.

Before building a new page or changing an approved page, present its exact content, layout, colors and fonts for approval. A direct user instruction to make a specific change authorizes that change. Do not seek the same approval twice.

## 1. Visual character

Quiet, premium, structured and editorial. Warm cream canvas; deep green surfaces; restrained clay accents; clear tables; generous open space. The hierarchy comes from typography, alignment and spacing. Do not introduce gradients, shadows, decorative fitness imagery, emoji, unrelated icons or new colors without approval.

## 6. Component rules

### Panels and cards

Standard cards have a 7 pt corner radius, a 0.5 pt warm-neutral outline and a white or evergreen fill. Major colored panels use a 6–7 pt radius. Table header corners use 4 pt. Avoid exaggerated pill shapes.

Meal cards use approximately 4 mm internal padding, with 4 mm between cards. Other panels use 14–18 pt internal padding. Major sections usually have 7–10 mm breathing room, adjusted to fit the content.

### Tables

- Evergreen header, cream heading text; header height 29 pt.
- White and cream alternating rows.
- Labels left-aligned; numeric values right-aligned.
- Header text inset 9 pt; numeric values inset 11 pt from the right edge.
- Standard body row heights range from 30 to 39 pt according to content.
- Tracker rows are 8 mm high to support handwriting.
- Wrap long labels; never allow text to collide with adjacent columns.
- Do not use heavy grids or vertical borders everywhere.

Approved column proportions:

| Table | Column widths |
|---|---|
| Body composition | Metric 65% / Result 35% |
| Macros | Nutrient 40% / Target 30% / Energy 30% |
| Goal map | Checkpoint 34% / Weight 26% / Loss 40% |
| Hydration | Time 65% / Amount 35% |
| Weekly menu | Menu 10% / Day type 25% / Protein 33% / Starch 32% |
| Weight tracker | Week 12% / Date 30% / Weight 28% / Adherence 30% |
| Feedback tracker | Week 12% / Hunger 23% / Energy 23% / Notes 42% |

Default approved quotation:

“Discipline is the bridge between goals and accomplishment.”

Set it over two centered lines as in the master. Do not invent an attribution. Long names or translated titles may require an approved line-break adjustment.

### B. Body composition

Title, client name and scan details; then a simple two-column metrics table. Use only supplied measurements. No invented normal ranges or classifications. Do not automatically restore the deleted supporting paragraph or separate segmental-analysis page.

### C. Nutrition targets — revised master

Use this order:

1. Evergreen calorie-target panel, top 150 pt, height 125 pt. Short uppercase label, 24 pt calorie figure, 10 pt day-type and meal-structure lines.
2. Energy section heading at baseline 313 pt.
3. White calculation card, top 330 pt, height 133 pt. Resting energy and daily expenditure in two balanced columns; labels 10 pt, values 18 pt. Short formula lines below, 10 pt with 15 pt leading.
4. Macronutrient heading at baseline 502 pt; table top 520 pt, 37 pt body rows.
5. Evergreen fibre strip, top 723 pt, height 34 pt; 10 pt cream text.

Numbers and equations must match the client. Rahma's 1,400 kcal and 95 g protein are not design defaults.

### D. Goal map

Three-part evergreen metric strip; milestone table; white check-in instruction panel. Adapt the goal and checkpoint count. Do not promise a fixed completion date. The deleted timeline paragraph is not part of the standard layout.

### E. Hydration

Green target panel; timing table; brief training guidance; two rows of outlined glass icons where useful. Recalculate the target and icon count for the client. Do not restore the deleted practical-check paragraph automatically.

### F. Weekly meal overview

Title; evergreen summary panel; menu table; concise usage instructions. End with open space. **Do not restore the removed portion-measurement box.** Meal-day labels follow the client's schedule; a seven-day/four-meal structure is not mandatory for other clients.

### G. Daily meals

- Title: **Day [number] — [day type]**.
- Total strip starts at y = 150 pt.
- Default layout: two-line green strip, height 55 pt; 12 pt calorie line and 9 pt macro line.
- The existing Day 1 five-column strip is an approved alternate, height 49 pt, using 7 pt labels and approximately 12 pt values. For a new plan, choose one strip style at approval and use it consistently.
- First meal card top: y = 226 pt.
- Three-item card: 111 pt high; four-item card: 124 pt high; expand when needed.
- Heading band: 26 pt high, cream fill.
- Meal name: left, 14 pt Newsreader; calorie value: right, 10 pt Manrope SemiBold in clay.
- First ingredient baseline: card top + 45 pt. Subsequent ingredients: +15 pt.
- Macro baseline: card bottom − 12 pt, 8 pt Manrope.
- Gap between cards: 4 mm.

Use the client's approved portion descriptions. Keep a precise internal calculation basis even when the page uses count-based portions. Do not report exact nutrition for undefined portions. Seek approval for any essential clarification before final assembly. No supplementary paragraph or subtitle should reappear solely because an old layout note mentions it.

### H. Progress tracking

Title; green check-in-day band; concise weighing instructions; weight/adherence table; hunger/energy/training table; rating definitions; notes line. Eight rows are a reusable design component, not a mandated program duration. Sunday is Rahma's preference, not a fixed rule for all clients. Keep sufficient writing space; add a page when needed rather than crowding the footer.

## 8. Fixed design vs personalized content

Fixed: brand assets, palette, font families, heading hierarchy, page margins, header/footer alignment, panel shapes, table styles and restrained visual tone.

Personalized: identity, sex, assessment data, goals, calculations, intake, protein target, fibre/hydration, meal count, food selection, portion units, training/rest days, language, check-in day, timeline, tracking metrics and document length.

The old separate personal-profile and segmental pages are excluded from the default sequence. Add specialist pages only when useful and explicitly approved. Never add filler to reproduce fourteen pages.

## 9. Production and approval checks

1. Use the latest user-edited content and accepted numerical corrections.
2. Remove deleted paragraphs even if stale layout instructions still refer to them.
3. Verify client identifiers, quantities, sums, units and dates independently of design.
4. Include both original logo components on every page.
5. Embed fonts and retain selectable text in the PDF.
6. Render and inspect every page for clipping, collisions, missing glyphs and misalignment.
7. Check numerical table alignment and footer consistency.
8. Confirm all approved removals remain absent.
9. Deliver only after the approved changes have been applied and verified.
10. Save the final artifact and preserve the existing file's identity when editing it.
